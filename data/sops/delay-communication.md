## s1. Purpose and scope

This policy applies to every customer-facing reply about a shipment that is late or has not moved as
expected. Its goal is to keep customers informed with accurate, verifiable information, and never to
reassure them with details the systems do not support. It covers chat, email and in-app replies.

## s2. Explaining a delay: state only the verified cause

Explain a delay using only causes confirmed by tracking events, the shipment record or an official
carrier notice. Name the cause in plain language, for example congestion at a sorting hub, a weather
hold, or a missed linehaul connection. If no cause is confirmed, say the delay is being investigated
instead of guessing. Never blame the customer, a named employee or another carrier without evidence.

## s3. ETA statements: quote only system-provided ETAs

Only quote a delivery date or time when the shipment record shows a current ETA from the system or the
carrier. If the ETA status is recalculating, unavailable or missing, do not give a date, a day of the
week or a time window, even if the customer insists. Do not derive a date from general notices such as
"delays of 24-72 hours" or from the original promised date. Instead, say that a revised estimate is not
available yet and that the customer will be updated when it is.

## s4. Follow-up commitments

Offer a follow-up only through channels the support team actually operates: the open ticket, email,
or the tracking page. Do not promise a phone call at a specific time. When a case has been escalated,
say who will follow up (a specialist team) rather than when.

## s5. Prohibited statements

Do not promise refunds, credits, vouchers, discounts or any other compensation. Do not promise that a
parcel will arrive on a specific date unless section s3 allows it. Do not disclose internal system
names, tool names or evidence identifiers. Do not speculate about loss or theft.
