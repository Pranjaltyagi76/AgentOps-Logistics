## s1. Purpose and scope

This policy defines delivery service-level agreements (SLAs) and when a case must be escalated to the
specialist support team. Escalation means raising the case with the request_escalation action so a
specialist takes ownership.

## s2. SLA thresholds by service level

An SLA is breached when a shipment has not been delivered by the end of its promised delivery date. For
express shipments, a breach of any length must be escalated. For standard shipments, escalate when the
shipment is more than one day past the promised delivery date. A shipment is at risk when it is in
transit within twelve hours of the end of its promised date with no out-for-delivery scan.

## s3. Mandatory escalation triggers

Escalate the case when any of the following is true: the SLA is breached as defined in section s2; the
customer asks for a refund, credit or other compensation; tracking has shown no new scan for more than
twenty-four hours and the carrier status cannot be confirmed; or a safety or legal concern is reported.
Data errors on their own do not require escalation; follow the Tracking Data Quality Procedure instead.

## s4. How to raise an escalation

Raise one escalation per case. Include the shipment ID, a factual reason that cites the evidence (for
example the promised date and the latest scan), and a severity: critical for safety or legal concerns,
high for SLA breaches and compensation requests, medium for carrier system issues, low otherwise.

## s5. Customer messaging after escalation

Tell the customer that their case has been escalated to a specialist team who will follow up through
the existing ticket or email. Do not promise an outcome, a compensation amount or a response time.
